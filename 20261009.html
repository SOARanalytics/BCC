Order Up: Two Doubleheaders and a Chance of Rain

HCYP Blue, first among 101 teams in the data, comes to Cabin John on a clear Saturday. The Pirates come Sunday, if the sky over Germantown can be talked out of it.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Thursday, Oct. 8, 2026

Nobody orders a double-double expecting to stay clean. The thing is built to be a mess, and the pleasure lies in whether you finish it. BCC has ordered two this weekend. Saturday, late in the afternoon at Cabin John, brings a doubleheader against HCYP Blue, one game stacked on the other. Sunday brings another against the Pirates of Olney, provided the weather allows the kitchen to open at all.

The best kitchen in town

Start with the numbers, which will not improve by being ignored. HCYP Blue ranks first among 101 teams in the data. BCC ranks seventh, a respectable seat at a table where the head chair is a long way off. HCYP is 12–2 this fall, scoring 13 runs a game and allowing fewer than four. Its two losses came by a run to Gambrills and by five to Wild Bill, so the door is not welded shut. It is only heavy.

Two of its bats have 37 home runs between them across three seasons and strike out about as often as it snows in June. The club stole 88 bases in 14 games, a pace that suggests it regards the base paths as a public right of way.

BCC has met this team three times and lost three times: by five, by four, and, most recently in April, by one. That is a trend, if you squint, and baseball parents are practiced squinters.

The door is not welded shut. It is only heavy.

The soft spot is the staff. Two arms throw strikes; behind them the walks come in bunches. Hit those two early. Opponents have stolen on this club in 18 of 22 tries this fall, which sounds fine until you do the arithmetic and find it falls short of paying for the risk, so send runners only on a clean read.

Our analytics have BCC winning any one of these games a shade better than one time in three, with a typical score landing around 9–6 or 9–7 the other way. They make taking both ends about a 1-in-7 proposition, and taking at least one about 3 in 5. That last figure is worth a parent's hope, the way a small umbrella is worth carrying.

The old quarrel

Then comes Sunday, and the Pirates, whom this household has never learned to like and has never quite learned to beat in comfort. Five games on record: two wins, two losses and a tie, which is about as honest as a ledger gets without adding a column for feelings. There was an 8–8 tie, an 11–4 loss and a 5–4 squeaker along the way. The ratings place the Pirates eleventh in the data, four spots behind BCC, and their fall record is 5–7–1. The ratings have never been consulted when these two shake hands.

On Wednesday the Pirates met HCYP Blue and went home on the wrong end of a 21–6 score. That hands BCC two pieces of intelligence. The first is that nobody is safe. The second is that a pirate who has just been keelhauled is still a pirate, and usually arrives in a worse mood.

A pirate who has just been keelhauled is still a pirate.

The lineup walks and it runs: 60 steals in 13 fall games, and several hitters who reach base more than half the time. The staff is uneven, with two arms who find the zone and a long procession who do not; one of them has walked better than a third of the batters he faced. Be selective on the bases. Opponents are 62 for 69 against them, which is right at the break-even line, and that is the statistician's way of saying don't.

Our analytics call each game a coin flip with a little extra spin on BCC's side, a game that tends to finish near seven-all, and give BCC a little better than 3 chances in 4 of winning at least one.

The sky

Saturday evening is forecast clear at Cabin John, the kind of evening that gets a twelve-year-old squinting into the sun at the wrong moment and a parent remarking that it was a perfectly good day for a ballgame. Sunday is another matter. Germantown looks to have rain coming, and a rainout would postpone the quarrel with the Pirates without settling it. Several fine rivalries have been preserved that way.
