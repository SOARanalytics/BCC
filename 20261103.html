Veterans Day Tournament · Ripken Experience, Aberdeen, Md. · 12U · Field Preview

Thirteen Teams, Six States, and Most of Them Strangers

From Westchester County to Virginia Beach, a 12U field gathers in Aberdeen on Nov. 7 and 8. A guide to who is coming, who is good, which Saturday games are worth the walk between fields, and how Sunday is likely to shake out.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026

Most of the people in the bleachers this weekend will not know the team in the other dugout. That is the charm of the place.

The Ripken Experience in Aberdeen is a small town of baseball fields built to look like big-league parks, in the town where the Ripkens grew up. A boy from Port Chester, N.Y., can play at Citi Field before lunch and a boy from Georgetown, Del., can play at PNC Park after it, and neither will have to explain to anybody back home that the outfield wall was a little closer than advertised. The main field is Cal Sr.'s Yard, named for the father, and the championship game will be played there at 5 o'clock Sunday afternoon with no time limit, which is the closest thing to a compliment a tournament schedule can pay.

It is Veterans Day weekend. The boys will be playing a game. The holiday is named for people who did a good deal more than that, and it would not hurt for a few of the grown-ups to remember it somewhere between the second game and the drive home.

The shape of the weekend

Thirteen teams come from six states: three from Westchester County, two from New Jersey, one each from Pennsylvania and Delaware, three from Maryland and three from Virginia. Each plays two pool games. MP Colonials and Fielder's Choice get started against each other Friday afternoon; everyone else plays Saturday, across eight fields, between 8 a.m. and the late afternoon.

The top nine advance to the championship bracket. Eight and nine play at 8 a.m. Sunday for the right to meet the top seed. Three and six also play at 8. The rest of the quarterfinals go at 10:15, the semifinals at 12:30, and the final at 5. The top two seeds need three wins to take the title; whoever comes out of the eight-nine game needs four. Seeds 10 through 13 play off for consolation trophies, which are smaller but, by the testimony of every 12-year-old who has ever won one, still count.

For most of these teams, our analytics have never seen them play one another. The connections run through common opponents, and in a few cases through opponents of opponents. Where the trail is thin, the projections say so by being less sure of themselves.

The two at the top

BCC Big Train (Bethesda, Md.; 15–7 this fall) comes in as the favorite. It has scored 203 runs and allowed 113 this fall, and our analytics rate both its hitting and its pitching at the top of this field. They make it a near-certainty to reach the championship bracket and give it a little under half of all the titles in their simulated weekends. That is a lot for one team in a field of 13. It is also, plainly, not a majority.

Delaware Stallions (Georgetown, Del.; 5–2) are the other half of the conversation, and the most interesting team in the field. Their fall record is thin, only seven games, but they went 38–8 in the spring, and this fall they have scored 109 runs in seven games, better than 15 a game. One October Saturday they played three games against Delmarva Aces teams and scored 66 runs, two of the games ending 26–9 and 26–0. Our analytics rate their lineup the most dangerous in Aberdeen, ahead of anyone else's, and give them about one chance in four at the title. They lost twice in late October, by three and by six, so they are not unbeatable. They are simply the team nobody will want to see across the diamond on Sunday afternoon.

The contenders

Blasters Baker (Virginia Beach; 12–7–1) began the fall 0–4 and have gone 12–3–1 since, a turnaround that suggests either a team that figured something out in early September or a schedule that got kinder. Our analytics believe a good deal of the former. They are solid on both sides of the ball and give the Blasters better than nine chances in ten to make the bracket and about one in 16 to win it.

Chester County Titans (West Chester, Pa.; 17–3–1) have the best record in the field, by a distance. They have scored 233 runs in 21 games. And yet our analytics rate them well down the list, which deserves an explanation. The Titans have played almost entirely around southeastern Pennsylvania, against teams nobody else in this field has met, so there is very little to measure them against. A team that has beaten everyone in its own neighborhood has not yet been asked to play in anyone else's. They also come in having lost their last two, 13–8 and 10–1. Saturday will be the first real look, and it is a revealing one: the Titans play BCC at 12:30. Our analytics give them about one chance in 18 at the title. They would not be shocked to be wrong about this team in either direction.

Briarcliff Bears Blue (Briarcliff Manor, N.Y.; 3–4) are the field's great pitching-and-defense team, second only to BCC in run prevention by our analytics' reckoning, and one of the weaker hitting teams. They went 9–1 last fall. The curiosity is depth: they carry 18 players but have used only six pitchers this fall. On a Sunday that could ask for three or four games, that will be the thing to watch. About one in 20 to win it.

The Westchester delegation

Three of the teams come from the same stretch of Westchester County, and two of them know each other well.

MP Colonials (Mount Pleasant, N.Y.; 5–5) may be the hardest-luck team in the field. Four of their five losses this fall were by one run, including a 16–15 game that must have taken most of an afternoon. Their wins are close, too: 4–3, 3–2, 4–2. If you like baseball that comes down to the last at-bat, find wherever the Colonials are playing. They open Friday at 4 against Fielder's Choice, a game our analytics call a coin flip, and then meet the Blasters on Saturday morning.

Port Chester Pirates (Port Chester, N.Y.; 6–5–1) have played the Colonials three times this fall and lost two of them, 4–3 and 4–2, while winning the other 6–3. They are a low-scoring club at both ends: 55 runs for and 44 against in 12 games. Their Saturday morning game against SJ Titans Black is as close to an even proposition as the schedule offers. The two Westchester rivals are not in the same pool games, which means that if they meet again, it will be on Sunday, when it matters more.

The rest of the field

Wildcats Blue (Northvale, N.J.; 10–10) have scored 154 runs this fall and allowed 155. It is hard to imagine a more balanced ledger. They are favored in both pool games and have a good chance of a top-four seed, which our analytics put at better than even.

SJ Titans Black (Blackwood, N.J.; 9–13–1) went 16–6 last fall and have lost a run of close ones lately, by one, by two, by two. Better than their record.

Fielder's Choice (Purcellville, Va.; 7–6–1) have the most familiar résumé to anyone who has followed Maryland and Virginia baseball this fall; they have played several of the region's better clubs, including a 22–1 loss to HCYP Blue that they would probably prefer not to have mentioned. They beat Stars Jones twice in September, 19–2 and 7–5.

Rawlings Prospects (Crownsville, Md.; 7–11) are a merged team, part of it drawn from a Green Hornets group some local families will recognize, and they have won three of their last four. Among those was an 11–8 win over Annapolis Area Hammers (Davidsonville, Md.; 4–9), who have been on a hard run lately. Stars Jones (Fairfax, Va.; 6–10) appears to be a newly formed group with no history our analytics can find. All three are long shots for the bracket and good bets for a consolation trophy; our analytics put the Prospects as the likeliest winner of the second consolation final.

Saturday, worth the walk

Briarcliff Bears vs. Delaware Stallions, 10:15, Citi Field. The best pitching team against the best lineup. The Stallions are favored about two times in three, but if the Bears can keep that lineup quiet through the early innings, it becomes their kind of game.

Chester County Titans vs. BCC Big Train, 12:30, Cal Sr.'s Yard. The best record against the best rating, and the one game that will tell everyone what the Titans really are. Our analytics favor BCC about four times in five, and admit to being less certain than usual.

Blasters Baker vs. MP Colonials, 10:15, Wrigley. Near even, with a little lean to the Blasters. If it is close late, history says the Colonials will be in it to the last out, for better or worse.

Port Chester Pirates vs. SJ Titans Black, 10:15, PNC Park. The truest coin flip of the day.

Sunday

The most likely final, by a wide margin, is BCC against the Stallions, which turns up in a little over one simulated weekend in five. Our analytics favor BCC in that game about six times in ten. Nothing else comes close in likelihood; the next most common finals pair BCC with the Blasters, the Bears or the Titans, each well under one in ten.

Two things could unsettle the picture. The first is the Titans, about whom nobody knows enough. The second is pitching. Sunday's champion will need three or four wins in about nine hours, and teams that went through Saturday on one or two arms will feel it by the semifinals. Most of these clubs have used nine or ten pitchers this fall. The ones who have used fewer will be the ones whose coaches are counting on their fingers by lunchtime.
