How it works

  • Every half-inning in the warehouse (about 24,600) is one observation: runs scored by the batting team against the fielding team, with the pitcher who faced most of that inning on the mound.
  • Runs per half-inning follow a negative binomial. The log-mean adds the batting team's offense, subtracts the fielding team's run prevention, and adds the pitcher's deviation from his own staff, a home-field term, a fatigue term on his pitch count, and a random "team day" effect per game.
  • Team offense and defense follow a random walk across seasons (fall 2025, spring 2026, summer 2026, fall 2026). The data decide how much a team drifts, so there are no hand-set season weights.
  • Fitted by Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (numpy only, two chains). Each simulated tournament uses a different posterior draw, so the odds include uncertainty about how good each team is.
  • Pitcher usage copies what coaches in the warehouse actually did on 362 two-day tournament weekends: aces sit Saturday about a third of the time and are held near 35 pitches when they don't, starters go 40–60 pitches, a blow-up inning gets a pitcher pulled, and nobody goes past 85 for the weekend.
