Sykesville Wood Bat Tournament · 12U · Championship Sunday

The Arms Did What Was Asked. Now Comes the Old Business.

BCC allowed one run in two games, took the top seed and a first-round bye, and plays at 12:30 in sunshine. Elkridge and Olney are on the other side of the bracket.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Sunday, Oct. 18, 2026

On Friday this column said the weekend would belong to the pitchers. The pitchers, apparently, read it.

Ten innings on Saturday. Six hits allowed. One run. It was 47 degrees with a wind that made it feel like 35, the infield was wet under its rake marks, and BCC's staff spent the day throwing strikes as though the cold were somebody else's problem. Winfield Wolfpack managed one hit in a 9–1 loss. The Diamond Birds, who score 11 runs a game against most people, were shut out 8–0.

The bats, meanwhile, were supposed to be learning humility. They declined the lesson twice. BCC hit a home run in each game, with wood, in the cold, which was not in anyone's plan. Of the three teams with a real claim on this tournament, BCC was the only one to clear a fence in Saturday's box scores.

The bats were supposed to be learning humility. They declined the lesson twice.

The road

The reward is the top seed, a bye, and a late start. BCC plays at 12:30, by which time the frost will be gone and the forecast says 55 and sunny, the first day all weekend that a parent can sit in a folding chair without a blanket over the knees.

The semifinal opponent is the winner of the morning game between Green Hornets–Green and the Sykesville Cyclones' Navy team. The Hornets come in having scored 29 runs on Saturday, winning 10–7 and 19–5, and our analytics like them about two times in three to get through. The Cyclones won a 12–5 game and then a 2–1 game, which is a team showing it can win two different ways in one afternoon. Against the Hornets, our analytics have BCC winning about three times in four; against the Cyclones, about nine in ten.

The other side

Elkridge, the second seed, also has the morning off, and earned it. Its pitchers allowed three hits all Saturday and not one earned run. The hitting was another story: Elkridge beat the Diamond Birds 9–0 on two hits, which means it did not so much hit its way past them as stand at the plate and wait. Eight walks will do that. It is worth noting that BCC and Elkridge both shut out the same team on the same day, Both dugouts will have noticed.

Olney, the third seed, has to work for its place. It plays Winfield in the morning before it ever sees Elkridge. Olney's one Saturday result on the books was a 13–3 win built on five hits and nine walks, and its starter carried 68 pitches into the fourth inning to get it, which may matter by mid-afternoon. Winfield, for its part, lost to BCC and then beat the Cyclones' Red team for the second time in ten days. A habit is forming. Our analytics give Olney better than nine chances in ten against it.

Then Elkridge and Olney meet for the right to play in the final, and our analytics call that one near even, with the slightest lean toward Olney.

The old business

Last weekend's game against Olney was rained out. This column said at the time that a rainout would postpone the quarrel without settling it, and the weather has kept its end of the bargain: the quarrel is still here, and so, possibly, is the final. Elkridge brings history of a less pleasant kind. The Hurricanes, Orange edition, have met BCC three times and won all three: 5–2 last fall, then 10–6 and 13–3 on a single June weekend. Our analytics, which weigh everything since, make BCC a slight favorite this time. Nobody has told the record book. Either way, the last game of the weekend is likely to be against a team BCC already knows by heart.

Our analytics have BCC reaching the final about four times in five. Against Elkridge they call it a coin flip with a little extra spin on BCC's side; against Olney, nearer even than that. Title odds come to a little under half, the best in the field, and Elkridge and Olney sit at about one in four each. Those are the numbers. They have never once been consulted when these teams shake hands.
