For BCC, the road to Sunday starts with an old friend

Twelve teams, two days, one trophy. The Big Train arrive as a real contender in a field with a clear favorite and a crowded middle, and their road starts against the one opponent they can never quite shake.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Friday, Sept. 25, 2026

Somewhere around 5:30 on Sunday evening, two teams of 12-year-olds will line up on the chalk for the Gold bracket final, and nobody in the bleachers will be pretending not to be nervous. Whether BCC Big Train is one of them is roughly a one-in-four proposition, according to projections from our Analytics Division. In a field this deep, that's a very good place to start.

The Big Train are 8–4 this fall and have won about seven of every ten since last September. They don't overwhelm anyone. They grind: sturdy pitching, clean defense, a lineup that turns walks into runs. On this weekend, that makes them one of three or four teams with a legitimate shot.

Saturday: the old friends from Olney

The schedule did BCC no favors. The opener is Olney Pirates Henig, the team the Big Train can't seem to separate from. They tied 3–3 in May and still look like mirror images. Call it a coin flip with a little extra spin on BCC's side.

Henig runs. Constantly, cheerfully and very successfully. No team we've followed steals more, and holding runners has been BCC's soft spot all year. This one may come down to how often a Henig runner takes off for second, and how often BCC's battery makes him think twice.

Beat Henig and BCC is all but certain to play in the Gold bracket. Lose it and the afternoon becomes a lesson in tiebreakers.

Runs allowed is the first tiebreaker after record, so every run matters, even in a game that looks won. The nightcap, against Arlington Arsenal Blue, looks friendlier. Arsenal was terrific in the spring and has come out cold this fall. BCC should be a clear favorite, but a good lineup in a slump is still a good lineup, and slumps have a way of ending on somebody else's Saturday.

The field

Gambrills is the team to beat, and it isn't close. Nobody here keeps runs off the board better, and they make the Gold bracket in nearly every weekend our Analytics Division played out. BCC knows it firsthand: Gambrills swept the Big Train in a doubleheader last Sunday.

Olney Pirates Williams is the most intriguing team in the field, nearly unbeatable in the spring and around .500 this fall. We trust the longer résumé. Williams has the most dangerous lineup in the division, and a BCC–Williams bracket game would be about as even as baseball gets.

The crowded middle is where this tournament will probably be decided. Elkridge Orange and Wild Bill meet Saturday in a true toss-up. BCC lost to Wild Bill on Labor Day weekend, then beat them for a championship two days later. Henig belongs here too, which is why the opener carries so much weight.

Loudoun South Eagles, down from Aldie, are the team nobody wants in a 3–2 game. They don't score much and don't give up much, and they're the wild card for the last Gold spots. Arsenal, Maryland Jbats and Dig In Nation need a big Saturday. The Jbats and Dig In have barely played this fall, so take any read on them with a grain of salt. The host Shockers and the Ole Liners face the longest road, though either could make noise in Silver.

Sunday: where pitch counts come due

Every coach plays the same chess game on a two-day weekend: spend your best arms Saturday for a seed, or save them for the trophy. The teams that manage it best are the ones still playing at dinnertime. A top-two seed and the morning off would help BCC most, but that's about a one-in-five shot. More likely, the Big Train open Sunday as a slight favorite against Henig, Wild Bill or Elkridge. Win that, and the afternoon semifinal is, more often than not, Gambrills.

Win the opener, don't give away runs, and hand the ball on Sunday to arms that still have something left.

BCC's weekend starts with Henig, a team it tied in May and hasn't gotten away from since. Everything after that is just odds, and odds are what make it worth showing up.
