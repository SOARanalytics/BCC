Trick or Treat in Winchester: The Composites Are Back

BCC carries six straight tournament titles into a Halloween tournament on turf. Saturday's pool holds the Rakers, who steal everything, and the Coppers, who used to be the Copperheads.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Thursday, Oct. 22, 2026

Six tournaments, six titles. The streak has reached the age at which people stop mentioning it out loud.

Baseball is a superstitious business, and a Halloween tournament is no place to tempt fate. So this column will say only that BCC is headed to Winchester, Virginia, for a weekend called the Field of Screams, that the goal on Saturday is a top-four finish in pool play and a place in the gold bracket, and that our analytics like BCC in both of its pool games. Nobody is saying the word itself.

The fields are turf, which means no rake marks, no puddles and no excuses. The afternoons should reach the mid-60s. The nights will drop to near freezing, which should keep the ghosts honest and the parents in the hotel.

The lumber goes back in the closet

Last weekend was a wood bat tournament, and the wood did not go quietly. One BCC hitter homered twice. Another homered, came up again hoping for an encore, and broke his bat instead. This weekend the composites come back out of the garage, rested, warm and presumably a little offended at having been benched. It being Halloween, at least one boy can be expected to hold his up in the dugout the way a certain horror-movie hero named Ash once held up a shotgun, and announce to nobody in particular, “This is my boom-stick.” Pitchers in the Shenandoah Valley have not been warned. This column does not intend to warn them either.

“This is my boom-stick.”

The Rakers

Rakers Gold come in at 6–9 this fall, and the record is about right. The pitchers throw strikes, which is admirable, and the strikes get hit, which is less so; their earned run averages this fall sit between five and eight. The lineup has hit seven home runs, total, across three seasons. The best hitter in it is a genuine problem, a boy hitting better than .550 who strikes out about as often as a lighthouse moves.

What the Rakers do is run. They have stolen 102 bases in 15 games this fall, nearly seven a game, which suggests a team that regards first base as a waiting room. On turf, where a ground ball gets through the infield in a hurry, that matters. Hold runners. Throw strikes. Make them hit their way around.

Our analytics have BCC winning about three times in four.

The Coppers, formerly of the Copperheads

Then there is the matter of the costume. At a Halloween tournament everybody is entitled to one, and the Copperheads appear to have come as the Coppers. It is not much of a disguise. The same ten boys wear the same ten numbers they wore as the Copperheads last fall and the Copperheads Black last spring. Our analytics followed them through every change of address without breaking stride. A snake that drops a few letters is still a snake. It may simply have heard who was in the pool.

A snake that drops a few letters is still a snake.

Nobody should mistake the disguise for weakness. Under the old name this group went 15–1 last fall. Under the new one it is 8–6–1, and ranks 25th of the 119 teams in our data. Eight of its ten regulars are hitting .350 or better. The leadoff man almost never strikes out. In the last two weeks they beat Annandale twice and Chantilly twice, and earlier this fall they beat the Rakers 21–8, which tells you something about both teams. When they lose, though, they tend to lose big, and to good teams: by 16 to St. James, by eight to Reston, by 11 to Storm Black.

Their pitchers miss bats; their two busiest arms strike out better than one batter in four. They also walk about one in five. Work the count, make them throw strikes, and save the big swings for the one arm who does throw them. They run, too, 75 steals in 15 games, though nobody runs like the Rakers.

Our analytics give BCC about two chances in three, the closest game of the day, and put the chance of winning both at about even.
