Field of Screams · Winchester, Va. · 12U · Gold Bracket Sunday

The Boom-Sticks Went Boom. Now the Snake Wants a Rematch.

BCC went 2–0 on Saturday, hit five home runs and enters the gold bracket as the second seed. The Coppers come first. The Wolverines, who have not given up a run all weekend, are waiting at the end.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Sunday, Oct. 25, 2026

There is no column in a box score for "declined to continue." The Coppers found one anyway.

It was late Saturday afternoon, BCC led 11–1, and there was one out left to get. The Coppers looked at the scoreboard, looked at their dugout, and conceded it. Nobody could blame them. They had been in trouble since the first inning, when BCC scored seven runs before their starter could record a second out. Everything after that was paperwork.

Earlier that afternoon the same team had beaten Prime Time Aces 18–1. Won by 17, lost by 10, same Saturday. Friday's column suggested the Copperheads had changed their name to slip into the tournament unnoticed. On the evidence, they would now like to slip out of it the same way. They will not be allowed to. They are BCC's semifinal opponent today.

Won by 17, lost by 10, same Saturday.

Five home runs and a shrug

The composites, you may recall, were promised. They delivered. BCC hit two home runs against the Rakers and three against the Coppers, one boy accounting for a pair, and scored 23 runs in two games. The Rakers held on longer than the Coppers did and lost 12–6. They steal bases the way other people breathe, and on Saturday they managed two.

The thing that wins Sundays

Home runs make the car ride home. Pitch counts make the bracket.

BCC used five pitchers on Saturday. Not one of them threw more than 39 pitches, and the two who handled the Coppers needed 45 between them. Every arm BCC brought over the Blue Ridge is still available this morning, most of them close to fresh. If the day runs to two games, that is the edge, and it is not a small one.

The Coppers cannot say the same. Their most dependable strike-thrower worked 59 pitches against BCC yesterday, and the rest of the staff walks about one batter in five. Our analytics, which had this semifinal drawn up before a pitch was thrown on Friday, give BCC about two chances in three.

The home team

Across the bracket sit the Winchester Wolverines, the top seed, playing a few miles from their own kitchens. They have scored 40 runs this weekend and allowed none. That is 21–0 over Stars Poppert on Friday night and 19–0 over Alexandria Reds on Saturday night, in seven innings of baseball, total.

Forty to nothing is a number that makes a parent in the stands sit up. It is worth asking whom it came against. Poppert and the Reds were the two teams our analytics rated weakest in the pool; BCC would have been favored over either of them about 19 times in 20. Judging the Wolverines by those two games is a little like judging a dog's temperament by watching it eat a sandwich. The Coppers, a reminder, also looked splendid yesterday, for most of an afternoon.

This is a real team, though, and the best one BCC can meet here. The Wolverines started the fall 2–6, have gone 11–2–2 since, and lost 6–1 only last Sunday. Their arms are rested, because nobody has made them work. Whether that is an advantage or a question is the most interesting thing about the afternoon. If both teams get through, our analytics make BCC the favorite about two times in three, and put the title itself at about even.
