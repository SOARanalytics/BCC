They steal more than any team we have scouted, and we are the worst team at stopping it

445 steals in 60 games at 95% — roughly seven and a half a game, with five players over 40: Jonah M (72), Jack T (56), Ben K (51), Hunter C (50), Ty J (43). A 20% team walk rate keeps feeding the machine. Set against that, BCC gave up 195 steals in 35 games last fall and 12 in four games at the Labor Day tournament, with four different catchers rotating. This is the matchup that decides the game, and it is the one where we are weakest. Everything else on this card is secondary.

Bottom line

The second-best team on our schedule after Gambrills — #15, 43–16, and a 1.079 team OPS built on contact rather than power (13 homers in 60 games). We tied them 3–3 in May. What keeps this at 56% rather than worse is their staff: a 5.73 ERA and a 2.06 WHIP, with exactly one arm that misses bats. Their fall record (4–2) is honest — they beat a #66 team and an unrated one twice each, and lost twice to Rawlings Prospects Hicks, #7 in the pool.

At the plate
Two of them throw strikes; the rest do not. Kaden T is the real arm (3.47 ERA, 1.50 WHIP, 30% K, 62% strikes — and no earned runs in 5 fall innings) and Ben K throws 69% strikes with a 5% walk rate. Against those two, hit early. Against Jonah M (27% walks, 53% strikes) or Ty J (28%, 51%), take until they prove it.
Run
Green light. Opponents are 339 for 361 — 94% — against them. We should be running too.
Defense
The whole card. Slide-step everyone, hold Ty J and Ben K at the top, and expect them to go on first movement. Jonah M hits third with a 1.580 career OPS and 72 steals — if he is on, he is running.
