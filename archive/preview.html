Shock and Awe · 12U-A Gold Bracket

BCC wakes up to a wet field and a fresh bullpen

The Big Train took care of an old rival before the nor'easter arrived. They go into Sunday as the No. 3 seed with most of their arms untouched, which is more than several of their neighbors can say.

By the SOAR Analytics desk · Sunday, Sept. 27, 2026

Some rivalries get settled. This one got a firm reminder. BCC Big Train and Olney Pirates Henig came into Saturday with a 3–3 tie from May between them and a season's worth of looking alike, and BCC left the field with a 12–3 win and the look of a team that had finally found the difference.

It was not complicated. BCC's defense gave away nothing. The bats, quiet enough in recent weeks to be taken for furniture, woke up. And Henig, a club that has spent two seasons making other teams chase it around the bases, found itself on the wrong end of the same idea. Every mistake it made, BCC's runners took an extra 70 feet for their trouble. By the late innings it had the feel of a card game in which one player has noticed the other's tell.

Henig's coach had a good deal to say to his players afterward, and he said all of it. It was delivered with feeling and at length. If the two teams meet again this weekend, someone ought to find the man a heating pad.

Then the weather took over. A nor'easter came up the coast, the sky went the color of a wet sidewalk and the rest of Saturday went with it. BCC's second game, against Arsenal Blue, never started. One game, one win and a runs-allowed column that the tiebreaker committee could love: BCC comes out of it as the No. 3 seed.

The arms

Here is where the rain may have done BCC a favor. The Big Train needed only two pitchers on Saturday, and neither threw more than about 50 pitches. Every arm at the top of their staff comes into Sunday rested.

The rest of the Gold bracket was not so thrifty. Wild Bill won twice and used three pitchers to do it, and all three threw 50 pitches or more; one of them threw close to 70. Loudoun South leaned on one arm for nearly 80 pitches in a single game, which means he has almost nothing left for Sunday. Dig In Nation sent six pitchers to the mound across two games. Henig used five in its two. Only Gambrills, which also played just once, arrives as fresh as BCC.

On a day when everybody's staff is counting pitches, BCC is counting pitchers.

The Big Train are one arm short for the weekend. They have enough others that it should not matter much.

The road

The first pitch is scheduled for 11 against Dig In Nation, if the grounds crew can persuade the infield to stop behaving like a rice paddy. BCC will be at home and a clear favorite; our analytics have the Big Train winning that one about three times in four. Dig In played twice on Saturday. It lost badly to Henig, then led Arsenal 2–1 in the fifth when the rain came and called the game. The timing suited Dig In; it was starting to crack, and the weather got there before Arsenal did. It arrives with a lot of innings already on its arms.

Win, and BCC plays Loudoun South at 3. The Eagles beat Olney Williams 3–2 on Saturday to earn a bye, which tells you most of what you need to know about them: they don't score much, and neither do you. But the pitcher who got them there has spent most of his weekend already. BCC would be a solid favorite, about two in three.

That would bring a 7 p.m. final, and probably Gambrills, which plays Henig at 1 and would then face top seed Wild Bill. Gambrills swept BCC in a doubleheader a week ago and is still the team the model likes best. It is also the only team in the bracket as rested as the Big Train. A final against Gambrills would be close to a coin flip. A final against Wild Bill, after its long Saturday, would lean BCC's way.

Put it together and our analytics give BCC a little better than a one-in-four chance of winning the whole thing. Only Gambrills is higher. That is a long way from a sure thing, but it is a better number than the Big Train brought into the weekend, and most of the improvement came from one sunny game and one very rainy afternoon.

Then someone will say play ball. That will be the good part.
